2. Secret Recipe

Frozen SigLIP SO400M. Each folder name is a short phrase, fixed before any test image is scored: InsideCity is "city scene surrounded by buildings," OpenCountry is "open countryside," TallBuilding is "tall building," and the rest are the ordinary words, such as "kitchen" and "highway." Four templates wrap each phrase: "a photo of a {phrase}.", "a photo of the {phrase}.", "an image of a {phrase}.", and "a {phrase} scene." The four embeddings are averaged and L2-normalized. That vector is the class weight. SigLIP's logit scale multiplies the cosine. It does not change the argmax.

Why this recipe, before the score.

The assignment points at a foundation model, and Assignment 1 already measured why. A frozen ResNet-18 head reached 91.04%, while a from-scratch CNN reached 89.38%. At 1,920 labels, where the features come from matters more than the head. Here the label budget is one image per class, so the features have to come from SigLIP. The class names are the part of that model I am allowed to use without opening a second training image.

The one labeled photo is the one-shot branch. The photo and a horizontal flip are averaged, and the same two views are used for every test image. The equal-mix classifier is the normalized sum of the text vector and that photo vector. Equal weight was fixed in advance. With one legal label per class, there is no second image to search a coefficient on. The photo-only classifier drops the text vector. The submitted cache.pt keeps the text vectors. Both comparison rows are part of evaluate.py, so a fresh run reprints all three numbers.

4. Failure Analysis: the equal mix

What I tried. The class weight was the normalized sum of the prompt vector and the one support photo. This was the recipe I expected to submit, because it is the only way to spend the labeled photo without a validation search.

Why I expected it to work. SigLIP knows "kitchen" and "highway." It does not have a settled phrase for InsideCity, Street, Suburb, and TallBuilding, which share buildings and roads. A real photograph of the class looked like the way to break those ties.

What happened. 82.00% test, 3 points behind the text prompts. The photo alone was 68.75%. The checkpoint moved to the text vectors.

What I learned. One photograph is a noisy prototype of a scene. The three weakest classes of the selected model are InsideCity (2/25, 8%), OpenCountry (15/25, 60%), and TallBuilding (17/25, 68%). Suburb is 18/25. InsideCity is 23 of the 60 errors. Bedroom, Coast, Flower, Highway, Kitchen, LivingRoom, and Mountain are perfect. The flip fixes small pose changes. It does not invent a boundary the text encoder never had, and one InsideCity photo does not repair that overlap either.

5. Limitations

One foundation model and one support seed, so I cannot say whether another SigLIP size or another draw of the 16 photos would move the 3-point gap. The flip is the only test-time adaptation. The encoder is not fine-tuned. The practical balance on one free T4 is a frozen foundation model, four prompts, and a flip, rather than a classifier trained on 16 images. The model is large, the legal training set is 16 photos, and the compute is a few minutes after the checkpoint download. Spending those 16 labels on a new head would re-learn kitchens and highways SigLIP already has, and it would still miss InsideCity.
